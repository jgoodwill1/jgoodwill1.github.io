# Joshua Goodwill

**Computational plasma physics · Solar-wind turbulence · Magnetic reconnection**

I am a **Physics Ph.D. candidate at the University of Delaware (expected Spring 2027)**, working with **Prof. William H. Matthaeus**, and a former **CSES Graduate Research Fellow at Los Alamos National Laboratory** (advisor: Dr. Fan Guo). I study the dynamics of turbulent plasmas across kinetic and macroscopic scales. My work combines **spacecraft observations, particle-in-cell simulations, statistical analysis, and plasma theory** to understand how magnetic and flow energy are transported and converted in space plasmas.

My research focuses on **solar-wind turbulence**, **turbulent magnetic reconnection**, and **pressure–strain-mediated energy conversion**, with an emphasis on connecting physical interpretation to robust, reproducible computational analysis.

## Research directions

### Solar-wind turbulence and Parker Solar Probe

How do measurement scale, averaging, and local plasma conditions shape our interpretation of the near-Sun solar wind? I investigate turbulent correlation scales, switchbacks, Alfvénic transitions, and the relationship between observations and theoretical scaling arguments.

[Research overview →](/research/#solar-wind-turbulence)

### Magnetic reconnection and kinetic energy conversion

How does turbulence redistribute energy between fields, bulk flows, and particle internal energy? I investigate the spatial structure and statistics of pressure–strain interactions, reconnection regions, and scale-dependent energy-transfer diagnostics in kinetic simulations.

[Research overview →](/research/#magnetic-reconnection-and-energy-conversion)

### Kelvin–Helmholtz turbulence and spacecraft observations

I study turbulent boundary layers and plasma energization in magnetopause Kelvin–Helmholtz events, seeking physically consistent comparisons between MMS observations and kinetic simulations.

[Research overview →](/research/#kinetic-boundary-layers-and-mms)

### Reproducible computational plasma physics

My computational work includes high-performance simulation analysis, particle-in-cell data readers, xarray-based diagnostics, statistical filtering, and scientific Python workflows.

[Software and methods →](/software/)

## Research approach

**Observations ↔ Simulations ↔ Theory.** I aim to connect physically motivated measurements to first-principles simulations while documenting the computational methods and assumptions behind each result.
